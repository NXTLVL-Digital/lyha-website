Where to Start · No Program Needed

Get on the
ice first.

Before joining a team or signing up for a full program, most kids skate the public sessions at the rink. This page explains where to go, what to expect, and how to get your child on the ice for the first time.

Start in Lynchburg Compare Options

First choice: LaHaye Ice Center

LaHaye Ice Center is in Lynchburg, right on the campus of Liberty University. It is the home rink for LYHA, the Hill City Coyotes, and many Liberty hockey programs.

Public Skate

Any kid can walk in and skate during public sessions. You buy a skate ticket at the door. Full ice gear is recommended, but the minimum is gloves, helmet, and a stick. Most kids show up in their own gear, though a lot of rinks also offer skate rentals.

Open Youth Hockey

Open Youth Hockey is the public drop-in hockey session. Bring your stick, gloves, and helmet, skate out to a line on the glass, and throw your puck in. Any kid on the ice gets a turn. It is typically around $10 per player, though prices shift with the rink schedule. Open sessions are a great way for a kid to get used to skating in a hockey setting before any formal practice.

Address: 1971 University Blvd, Lynchburg, VA 24515

Phone: (434) 592-3953

Why ice time can be limited at LaHaye

LaHaye is the only rink in the area, and it supports more programs than there are ice hours in a day. During any given week you will find Liberty University men's and women's ACHA hockey, figure skating, Learn to Skate, adult hockey, youth hockey, and independent programs like FCA Hockey and Rusty Blades Ice Hockey Club all sharing the same sheet. That means public sessions can sell out, and the schedule shifts with the season.

Check the rink calendar before you go. The schedule changes by season and holidays often cancel sessions. If you are planning around a specific day, call the rink or check their posted schedule to confirm.

Second option: Lancerlot Sports Complex

If LaHaye is full or you want more weekly chances, Lancerlot Sports Complex in Vinton, VA (about 20 minutes from Lynchburg) is the next stop. Their facility includes a full-size rink and they list Public Skate, Stick & Puck, and Drop-In Hockey sessions on a weekly basis year-round.

Stick & Puck

Stick and Puck is a casual drop-in session. You buy in, grab a puck, and skate. It is a relaxed environment and a good fit for younger kids who want to get reps without organized drills.

Drop-In Hockey

Drop-In Hockey is the closest thing to a full shift. Players line up, the horn sounds, and you play. It is a step up from Stick and Puck in intensity but still open to anyone who shows up.

Address: 1110 Vinyard Road, Vinton, VA 24179

Schedule: Monday–Saturday 5am–10pm, Sunday 8am–10pm. Check their schedule page for session times.

What to bring

Most kids show up with their own gear. If you are just getting started, here is what you need:

Helmet A certified hockey helmet (with full face cage) is the only must-have.
Gloves Any ice hockey gloves are fine. Even thick snow gloves work for the first time on ice.
Stick A lightweight stick is recommended. Rinks usually sell sticks at the door if you forget one.
Skates Ice hockey skates. Rental boots are available at both rinks if needed.
Optional Shin pads, shoulder pads, helmet cage, a puck or two. Start with the basics and add more as you go.
Skate rental Both rinks offer skate rentals for $3–$5. Call ahead if you want to be sure they have your size.
